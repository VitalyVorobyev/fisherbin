# DS-TILT-DUAL-EXACT-COMPLEXITY — exact bit complexity of the tilt-DP dual beyond one nuisance direction

**Programme:** P7 (OP31) · **Opened:** 2 September 2026 · **Status:** parked; reopen only by explicit queue decision
**Descends from:** `AUDITS/AUDIT-DS-PRACTICAL-CERTIFIED-SOLVER-001.md` §16 (the DS19 audit's next dependency-blocking question)

## Goal

Settle the remainder of `OPEN-DS-TILT-DUAL-EXACT-COMPLEXITY`. For a rational
score table \(\{(s_{\psi i},s_{\lambda i})\}_{i\le N}\subset\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q^{d_\lambda}\),
positive rational weights, exact-\(K\) nonempty cells, and the tilted score
\(T_\beta=s_\psi-\beta\cdot s_\lambda\), the dual

\[
\hat d_N=\min_{\beta\in\mathbb R^{d_\lambda}}\hat v_K(T_\beta),\qquad
\hat v_K(T_\beta)=\max_{z\ \text{interval labeling of }T_\beta}\ \sum_{b=1}^K\frac{M_b(\beta)^2}{W_b}
\]

is convex and piecewise quadratic in \(\beta\), exactly evaluable at any
rational \(\beta\) in \(O(KN)\) after sorting with ties tolerated (DS19.1 plus
the audit's tie lemma), bit-polynomially minimizable to any certified
\(\varepsilon\) (DS19.2 with the audit's bit model), **exactly** minimizable in
polynomial bit complexity at \(d_\lambda=1\) for every \(K\) (audit §7.5), and
exactly minimizable in polynomially many *arithmetic* operations for every
fixed \(d_\lambda\ge2\) with \(K\) part of the input (Toledo 1993,
fixed-dimension parametric search with degree-2 comparisons). Two decidable
questions remain, and they are this packet:

1. **Fixed \(d_\lambda\ge2\), variable \(K\) — bit complexity.** Prove or refute
   that the exact minimizer \(\beta^*\) (an algebraic point) and the exact
   value \(\hat d_N\) are computable in polynomial *bit* complexity. The
   candidate route is to carry a root-separation bound for the conic
   breakpoint arrangement \(\{q_z=q_{z'}\}\) through the parametric search:
   every DP comparison is a sign test of a degree-2 polynomial in \(\beta\)
   with integer coefficients of polynomial height, so the question is whether
   the intermediate algebraic numbers generated by the search stay in a field
   of polynomial degree and height. "Done" is a theorem with an explicit
   \(\mathrm{poly}(N,K,\log H,\ d_\lambda\text{-dependent exponent})\) bound, or an
   exact instance family whose certified minimizer needs superpolynomial
   bits.
2. **Variable \(d_\lambda\) — exact statement or hardness.** Either extend the
   exact algorithm to \(d_\lambda\) part of the input, or exhibit a valid
   hardness obstruction (a reduction from a known hard problem to *exact*
   tilt-dual minimization with the interval-DP oracle, or a proof that the
   number of distinct DP-optimal labelings met by any exact algorithm must
   be superpolynomial in \(d_\lambda\)). "Done" is a theorem, a reduction, or
   a reduction of the question to explicitly named assumptions.

Certified \(\varepsilon\)-accuracy is *not* the question; DS19.2 already
settles it and nothing here may weaken that. "Exact" means an algebraic
minimizer and value with checkable comparisons — a crossings grid,
floating-point bisection, or "\(\varepsilon\) small enough" is not exact.

### Tier A (load-bearing for this packet) — questions 1 and 2

Tier A must reach a stop-condition verdict for question 1. Question 2 may
close as *reduced* provided the named assumptions are individually stated.

### Tier B (stretch, separately scored) — certified selection at the dual minimizer

The audit's deployment-facing remainder. At the exact minimizer \(\beta^*\)
the DP-optimal set \(D(\beta^*)\) can contain both a closing labeling (one
satisfying the saddle normal equation \(\beta^*I_{\lambda\lambda}(z)=I_{\psi\lambda}(z)\))
and a non-closing one, and a deterministic DP tie policy can return the
latter (`CE-DS-TILT-DUAL-TIE-MASK-001`; 362 of 6,688 exhaustive small integer
tables). Decide:

- whether membership of a closing labeling in \(D(\beta^*)\) is decidable in
  polynomial time (the audit observed that a second DP pass in the opposite
  perturbation order, or a normal-equation check on every member of a DP tie,
  removed the mask in every case seen — is that a theorem?); and
- whether a certified closing labeling on the DS9 regular domain is
  necessarily exchange-stable in the DS17 sense, or whether an exact
  counterexample separates "certified global in-bin optimum" from
  "exchange-stable".

Tier B carries its own stop condition and must not consume Tier A's budget.
It authorizes no compile-path change either way.

## Known blockers

- **The one-dimensional argument does not lift.** At \(d_\lambda=1\)
  breakpoints are roots of degree-2 univariate polynomials and Mignotte-type
  separation gives \(2^{-\mathrm{poly}}\) gaps. At \(d_\lambda\ge2\) the
  breakpoints form an arrangement of conics; the minimizer sits at a vertex
  of the arrangement, on a conic, or in the interior of a cell, and its
  coordinates lie in an algebraic extension whose degree must be bounded
  along the whole search, not just at the end.
- **Parametric search is arithmetic, not bit.** Megiddo (1983) and Toledo
  (1993) bound operations over the reals with degree-\(\le2\) comparisons.
  Each comparison resolves the sign of a polynomial at an algebraic point;
  bounding the *bits* of those points requires either exact algebraic-number
  arithmetic with degree control (Basu–Pollack–Roy style) or a bespoke
  argument for this DP's specific quadratics. Do not cite an arithmetic
  bound as a bit bound.
- **Ties are structural, not accidental.** The DP argmax set \(D(\beta)\) is
  set-valued on a measure-zero set that contains the minimizer generically
  (\(\beta^*\) is where active quadratics cross). Every exact algorithm must
  handle the tie-order lexicographic DP (value, one-sided derivative,
  curvature) exactly; the audit's derivative policy is proved only at
  \(d_\lambda=1\). Directional derivatives at \(d_\lambda\ge2\) need their own
  tie orders per direction.
- **Envelope lower bounds do not transfer.** Carstensen (1983) and
  Gajjar–Radhakrishnan (2019) bound parametric shortest-path envelopes; the
  interval DP is a special grouping DP and parametric search shows the
  envelope need not be materialized. A superpolynomial envelope is *not* a
  hardness result for question 1.
- **Hardness must be a valid reduction.** OP11 already forbids importing
  \(k\)-means or D-optimal subset-selection hardness without a reduction. A
  hardness claim for variable \(d_\lambda\) must reduce a known hard problem
  to *exact* minimization with this oracle, respecting positive rational
  weights and exact-\(K\) nonempty cells.
- **Nuisance singularity.** Common empirical nuisance-null directions may be
  quotiented before optimization (registered assumption); after quotienting,
  coercivity holds with the audit's radius. Do not treat singular
  \(I_{\lambda\lambda}\) as a bug — it is a lower-dimensional instance.
- **Tier B: value versus labeling.** `CE-DS-INTERVAL-SEED-UNSTABLE-001` and
  `CE-DS-NONCENTERED-POPULATION-CUT-UNSTABLE-001` show DP labelings need not
  be exchange-stable in general; a *certified* labeling is a global in-bin
  optimum, which is the only reason stability is plausible. State the
  domain (DS9 regular, exact-\(K\)) and the move set explicitly.
- **No sample centering.** Uncentered second moments about the score-space
  origin throughout; the DS18 audit's H1 is the standing warning.

## Recommended starting points

- **The audit's algorithm and its proof.**
  `AUDITS/AUDIT-DS-PRACTICAL-CERTIFIED-SOLVER-001.md` §7.2 (tie lemma), §7.4
  (coercivity radius, bit model), §7.5 (\(d_\lambda=1\) exact algorithm with
  the explicit height and separation bounds \(H\), \(\delta\)), §10. The
  instrument `py/audit_ds_practical_certified_solver.py` implements
  `exact_dual_min_1d` with exact \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)\) arithmetic (`QSqrt`),
  one-sided derivatives from perturbation tie orders, and the certificate
  \(D^-(\beta^*)\le0\le D^+(\beta^*)\). Extend it — do not rewrite it — for a
  \(d_\lambda=2\) exact prototype; keep `fractions.Fraction` for everything
  claim-relevant.
- **Question 1, candidate proof.** Within one cell of the tilt-ordering
  arrangement the dual is the max of polynomially many convex quadratics in
  \(\beta\in\mathbb R^{d_\lambda}\) (bounded by the number of interval labelings
  of a fixed order, \(\binom{N-1}{K-1}\) — polynomial only for fixed \(K\), so
  do **not** enumerate). Toledo's search resolves the comparisons needed to
  simulate the DP at the unknown \(\beta^*\); the bit question is whether the
  algebraic point maintained by the search can be represented with degree
  bounded by a function of \(d_\lambda\) alone. Try: (i) at \(d_\lambda=2\), the
  minimizer is the vertex of one quadratic, a point on one conic, or the
  intersection of two conics — degree \(\le4\) over \(\mathbb Q\) — and a
  resultant-based separation bound for the arrangement gives an isolating
  box; (ii) show the search touches only points of the same type; (iii)
  generalize by induction on \(d_\lambda\) with degree \(\le 2^{d_\lambda}\).
  Every step must be a stated lemma; measure the bit growth on the
  instrument before proving.
- **Question 2, both directions.** Exact side: does the certified-\(\varepsilon\)
  bracket plus a separation bound give exactness by rounding, with
  \(\varepsilon\) polynomial-bit in \(d_\lambda\)? That needs a separation bound
  *uniform* in \(d_\lambda\), which is the crux. Hardness side: look at
  \(K=2\) first — a single cut — where \(\hat v_2(T_\beta)\) is the best
  two-interval split of a linear functional of \(\beta\); exact minimization
  over \(\beta\in\mathbb R^{d_\lambda}\) with \(N\) points resembles minimizing a
  piecewise-quadratic over a hyperplane arrangement. Check whether known
  hardness of exact optimization over arrangements (or of computing the
  exact value of a max of \(\binom N2\)-many quadratics) applies with this
  oracle; check the fixed-parameter literature (parametric search in
  variable dimension) before claiming a gap.
- **Falsify first** (`protocols/numerical.md`, exact rationals): on
  \(d_\lambda=2\) tables with \(N\le 8\), \(K\in\{2,3\}\), compute the exact
  minimizer by brute force over arrangement candidates (conic vertices,
  vertex-on-conic, quadratic vertices) with algebraic arithmetic; record the
  algebraic degree and bit size of \(\beta^*\) and \(\hat d_N\) as \(N\) grows.
  Superpolynomial growth in bits at fixed \(d_\lambda=2\) is the refutation
  target; degree \(>4\) at \(d_\lambda=2\) is a counterexample to the
  candidate proof's step (i). Attack ties, duplicate rows, singletons, and
  singular nuisance blocks — the audit's catalogue in
  `AUDITS/artifacts/AUDIT-DS-PRACTICAL-CERTIFIED-SOLVER-001/ceiling.json`
  names the adversarial families.
- **Tier B.** Start from `CE-DS-TILT-DUAL-TIE-MASK-001` and the audit's
  `tie_mask_search`. Formulate the closing-member test as a decision problem
  on \(D(\beta^*)\): the DP optimal set at a fixed tilt is an interval-labeling
  polytope's vertex set restricted by ties, and the normal equation is linear
  in the cell moments — check whether "some DP-optimal labeling closes" is a
  polynomial DP with an extra state (the residual
  \(\beta^*I_{\lambda\lambda}-I_{\psi\lambda}\), which is additive over cells).
  For exchange stability, test every certified labeling on the audit's 54
  closed tables against all single-row moves with exact gains.
- **Prior art to triangulate** (`protocols/literature.md`, before proving):
  Toledo 1993 (`Toledo-1993`), Megiddo 1983 (`Megiddo-1983`), Cohen–Megiddo
  1993, Basu–Pollack–Roy (algorithms in real algebraic geometry; bit bounds
  for sign determination), Mignotte and Davenport–Mahler root separation,
  Grønlund et al. 2017 (`Gronlund-etal-2017`) and Wang–Song 2011
  (`Wang-Song-2011`) for the fixed-tilt DP, Carstensen 1983
  (`Carstensen-1983`) and Gajjar–Radhakrishnan 2019
  (`Gajjar-Radhakrishnan-2019`) for envelope lower bounds, and parametric
  optimization in fixed versus variable dimension (Agarwal–Sharir, Chan).
  An empty result is a search gap, never novelty.

**Tier A** — one of:

1. **Proved.** Exact polynomial-bit minimization for fixed \(d_\lambda\ge2\)
   with \(K\) part of the input, as a theorem with an explicit bound; and
   question 2 proved, reduced by reduction, or reduced to named assumptions.
2. **Disproved.** A serialized exact instance family on which any exact
   minimizer or value requires superpolynomial bits at fixed \(d_\lambda\), or
   a valid hardness reduction for variable \(d_\lambda\).
3. **Reduced** to individually listed unresolved assumptions, each named with
   the object it constrains (for instance "degree control of the parametric
   search's maintained point").

attempted / budget exhausted" with partial results recorded. Tier B closing
as *reduced* does not block a Tier A closure.
