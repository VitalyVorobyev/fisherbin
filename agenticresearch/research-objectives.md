# Long-term research directions

Ordered by how directly they grow out of what the project already knows:

1. The declared open problem: error propagation from estimated scores to retained information. Chapter 13 states it precisely — given a score estimator with stated uniform or MSE error, bound the induced error in cell moments, criterion value, boundary positions, and truly retained Fisher information; find conditions under which the reported surrogate is conservative. This is the project's flagship theory question. A plausible attack: matrix perturbation of the log-det objective + stability of D-optimal Voronoi boundaries under score perturbation, composed with known classifier excess-risk → ratio-error rates. Even a one-model result with explicit constants would upgrade the ch13 ladder from "suggestive measurement" to "instance of a theorem." 2. Finite-sample uncertainty for retention itself. Every retention number the library reports is a point estimate. Influence-function or bootstrap-based confidence intervals for geometric_mean_retention — with the subtlety that hard assignment makes the functional non-smooth at cell boundaries — would let users report retention with error bars. This pairs naturally with #1.

3. High-resolution asymptotics: retention as K → ∞. Classical quantization has Zador-type rate theory for distortion; the analogue for Fisher information loss of an optimal K-cell partition (rate in K, dimension dependence, constant in terms of the score distribution) appears open and would tell users how many bins they fundamentally need. The exact scalar DP solver gives you ground truth in 1-D to conjecture against.

4. Strengthening ratio closure into a test family. Closure is documented as necessary-not-sufficient. There is a natural family of moment identities an exact ratio must satisfy (closure is the zeroth; score-orthogonality relations like E[s] = 0 under the reference measure are the first). A systematic hierarchy with power analysis would turn the current diagnostic into a calibrated goodness-of-fit test for estimated ratios — publishable on its own and immediately useful in the FlowCyt audit.

5. Robustness of the partition to θ₀. Everything is local at θ₀, and θ₀ is itself estimated in practice. Two questions: how fast does retention decay as the true parameter moves away from the design point (a computable diagnostic today), and can one optimize worst-case retention over a θ₀-neighborhood (a minimax design problem, connecting to robust optimal design of experiments)? The second likely changes the optimizer; the first is just a report.

6. The multivariate profiled upper problem. efficient_score_bound certifies one interest column and refuses the multivariate case rather than approximate it. A multivariate exact solver is probably hard (the scalar case leans on interval structure), but a certified convex relaxation with a stated gap would be honest by the project's standards and is a self-contained research problem.

7. Bridging to simulation-based inference. The score-from-classifier machinery is exactly the SALLY/SALLINO setting. A benchmark study — information retained by ScoreQuant's hard bins versus learned continuous summaries on standard SBI benchmark problems — would position the library in that community and likely surface new requirements (which then justify backlog items like samplers).
