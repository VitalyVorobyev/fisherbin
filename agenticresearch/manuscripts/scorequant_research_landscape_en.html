ScoreQuant research landscape · August 2026

Optimal quantization for preserving Fisher information

A history of ideas from D-optimal design and score-function quantization to modern inference-aware categorization — and a careful map of where our contribution may fit.

The central message is that this is not a single unified field, but the intersection of at least four research traditions. The literature closest to our formulation has developed score-based quantization and trace Fisher information extensively, while the HEP community has developed inference-aware categorization. What is not yet apparent in the literature we found is a ready-made theory of hard multivariate quantization with a full-matrix log-det Fisher objective, especially with exact finite-sample exchange geometry.

1. The field in one picture

Our question sits at the intersection of optimal experimental design, statistical quantization, geometric clustering, and inference-aware optimization of categories.

Optimal experimental design

D-, A-, E-, and Ds-optimality, log-det information, equivalence theorems, and sensitivity functions. This is where the language of “maximize the determinant of an information matrix” was established.

→

Quantization for estimation

How to transmit only a finite number of bits while losing as little parameter information as possible. This line introduced score-function quantizers and direct comparisons of Fisher information before and after quantization.

→

Our intersection

A deterministic hard quantizer of multivariate score space, a small number of bins, objective \(\log\det I_{ m bin}\), a deployable partition of the space, and exact finite exchange moves.

Clustering & vector quantization

Lloyd, Hartigan, Voronoi diagrams, determinant-based clustering criteria, and global complexity. This tradition provides the language of centroids, local relocations, and partition geometry.

→

Inference-aware categorization

INFERNO, SALLY/SALLINO, ThickBrick, and GATO/BOBR. The goal is to learn summaries or bins directly for the statistical sensitivity of the downstream analysis.

→

Practical library

The input may be precomputed scores, a score oracle implemented as a function, or a classifier-based proxy. The API should distinguish a sample-level partition from an inductive quantizer that can assign bins to new observations.

TraceAfter Fisher whitening, it reduces to weighted k-means; the geometry is well understood.
D / log-detBalances all information directions and is naturally related to the volume of a confidence ellipsoid.
DsFocuses on parameters of interest in the presence of nuisance parameters; the finite geometry becomes more difficult.
Significance / likelihoodHEP-oriented objectives, often differentiable and task-specific.

2. History: how four lines gradually converged

1960
Optimal design

Kiefer & Wolfowitz: D-optimality acquires a testable geometry

The equivalence theorem links D- and G-optimal design and turns the log-det objective into a local sensitivity condition. For our story this is an important ancestor: the same matrix nonlinearity \(I^{-1}\) appears as a local measure of quality.

Direct PDF · DOI

1967–71
Clustering

Friedman–Rubin, Marriott, Scott–Symons: determinant criteria in cluster analysis

Invariant and likelihood-ratio criteria based on determinants of scatter matrices appear. This is a close mathematical relative, but the classical objectives typically minimize within-cluster scatter or optimize a likelihood ratio rather than the Fisher between-cell matrix of a quantized score.

Scott–Symons PDF · Friedman–Rubin DOI · Marriott DOI

1973–81
Optimal design

Whittle and Ds-optimality

Whittle formulates a general equivalence theorem for concave design criteria; Näther & Reinsch later develop Ds-equivalence, including singular cases. This is important for a nuisance-parameter extension of our problem, but the optimization variable there is a design measure rather than a hard quantizer.

Whittle publisher page · Näther–Reinsch DOI

2005–06
Quantization for estimation

Venkitasubramaniam, Tong & Swami: score-function quantizers

The problem of optimizing quantization for distributed estimation through Fisher information is stated directly. Score-function quantizers serve as an optimal or benchmark structure. This is critical prior art: the general idea “quantize the score while preserving Fisher information” is not new.

Direct PDF · DOI

2012–13
Quantization for estimation

Farias & Brossier: high-resolution scalar theory

For a scalar parameter they derive the asymptotic Fisher-information loss, optimal interval density, and adaptive schemes. This is a deep one-dimensional theory, but it does not solve finite-K hard partitioning of multivariate score space with a matrix-determinant objective.

Direct PDF · arXiv

2018–20
Simulation-based inference

Brehmer, Cranmer and collaborators: the score as a learned summary

SALLY/SALLINO and related work establish the practical role of the local score in likelihood-free inference. MadMiner makes this line available as a software workflow. This closes an important practical gap: the score need not be analytically available; it can be estimated from simulations or classifier-like surrogates.

Direct PDF · arXiv

2018
Quantized Fisher geometry

Barnes, Han & Özgür: geometric characterization of Fisher information after quantization

The trace of Fisher information after finite quantization is expressed through conditional means of the score. For one-bit Gaussian location they solve an extremal problem and obtain a half-space quantizer. This is probably the closest modern theoretical predecessor of our score-space formulation.

Direct PDF · DOI

2019
Inference-aware ML

de Castro & Dorigo: INFERNO

A neural summary is trained directly against a differentiable approximation of statistical uncertainty in a binned likelihood. This is an important conceptual precedent: optimize the downstream inference objective rather than a proxy loss such as classification accuracy.

Direct PDF · arXiv

2021
Categorization

Matchev & Shyamsundar: ThickBrick

Event selection and categorization are optimized for signal significance, with an explicit connection to Lloyd-style iteration. Algorithmically this is close, but the objective and category structure are HEP-specific and do not coincide with full Fisher log-det score quantization.

Direct PDF · arXiv

2022–23
Quantization theory

Berkan Dülek: optimal sufficient-statistics-based quantizers

For exponential families, the existence of a deterministic K-level quantizer depending only on sufficient statistics is proved, together with a convex-polytope optimal partition for maximizing trace Fisher information. This sharply limits possible novelty claims about geometry: convex or polyhedral score quantizers are already known.

Publisher / DOI

2026
Modern bin optimization

Erdmann, Kasaraguppe & Mausolf: Learning to bin

GATO and BOBR directly optimize multidimensional hard-like categories of classifier discriminants for expected significance, using GMM boundaries, differentiable training, or Bayesian optimization. This is the closest modern software competitor at the level of “optimize bin shape in a multidimensional discriminant space.”

Direct PDF · arXiv

2026
ScoreQuant — current contribution hypothesis

Full-matrix D-optimal hard score quantization

The current project derives an exact rank-two finite move, a closed-form determinant gain, monotone Hartigan-style exchange, and the D-specific implication “1-exchange stable ⇒ self-consistent \(I^{-1}\)-Voronoi.” It also explores exact fixed-\((d,K)\) search, branch-and-bound, population purification, and extensions to Ds/E criteria. These points still require a dedicated publication-grade prior-art review.

3. Key authors and why they matter

J. Kiefer · J. Wolfowitz · P. Whittle

Optimal design

Sources of the modern language of D-optimality, sensitivity functions, and equivalence theorems. They are essential for avoiding accidental rediscovery of general concave matrix-optimization theory.

H. P. Friedman · J. Rubin · F. Marriott · A. J. Scott · M. J. Symons

Determinant clustering

They show that determinant criteria in partition optimization have a long history. Any novelty claim around “determinant clustering” therefore has to be very narrow.

P. Venkitasubramaniam · L. Tong · A. Swami

Score-function quantization

Key predecessors of direct quantizer optimization through Fisher information in distributed estimation.

R. C. Farias · J.-M. Brossier

Scalar Fisher-optimal quantization

High-resolution asymptotics, optimal interval density, and adaptive quantizer design for scalar estimation.

L. P. Barnes · Y. Han · A. Özgür

Geometric quantized Fisher information

The closest theoretical bridge to a multivariate score formulation: trace Fisher information through conditional score means and geometric extremal results.

Berkan Dülek

Sufficient-statistic quantizers

Convex-polytope optimality for a trace criterion in exponential families. An important boundary marker: polyhedral quantizer geometry is already known.

Johann Brehmer · Kyle Cranmer · Gilles Louppe et al.

Simulation-based inference

Score estimation and learned summaries make a score-space approach practical even when the likelihood is not analytically available.

Pablo de Castro · Tommaso Dorigo

INFERNO

An early prominent example in which a differentiable machine-learning objective is explicitly subordinated to downstream statistical inference quality.

Konstantin Matchev · Prasanth Shyamsundar

Optimal categorization

ThickBrick: iterative optimization of selection/categories with a Lloyd-like structure and a public Python implementation.

Johannes Erdmann · Nitish Kasaraguppe · Florian Mausolf

Learning to bin

A recent 2026 line: flexible multidimensional bins with differentiable or Bayesian optimization of significance.

5. Where our contribution may lie

The most defensible positioning is not to claim a new problem class as a whole, but to separate established ingredients from a new exact D-specific layer.

Problem formulation

Hard quantization of multivariate score space with a Fisher objective is historically close to known literature. This is not the strongest place to build a novelty claim.

prior art strong
D-objective

Using the full matrix \(\log\det I_{bin}\) rather than trace changes the geometry: the metric depends on the currently retained information and upweights weak information directions.

potentially distinctive
Exact finite move

Moving one weighted observation produces a rank-two update \(\Delta I=lpha uu^ op-eta vv^ op\), while the determinant ratio reduces to a 2×2 determinant.

not found directly
Exchange ⇒ Voronoi

If a finite partition is stable against all admissible one-point moves, then it is already a strict self-consistent Mahalanobis–Voronoi partition in the metric \(I_{bin}^{-1}\). The converse is false.

best theorem candidate
Monotone solver

A Hartigan-style exact exchange algorithm accepts only moves with positive exact log-det gain; the objective is therefore monotone and finite-sample termination is guaranteed.

algorithmic contribution
Global certification

The Voronoi structure reduces the search space; fixed-\((d,K)\) enumeration and refinement-based branch-and-bound provide routes to exact/global certificates.

needs complexity review
Beyond D

For A- and profiled Ds-optimality, the strong finite exchange→Voronoi geometry already admits counterexamples; the E-case remains less clear.

boundary result
Software architecture

A general API separates an observed-sample partition from a deployable space quantizer and supports an explicit score oracle, precomputed scores, and a classifier-derived proxy.

practical differentiation
A formulation that is defensible already
Not “we invented optimal Fisher binning,” but: we study the exact finite-sample and population geometry of full-matrix D-optimal hard quantization in multivariate score space, and build a solver that exploits this D-specific structure.

6. Main open questions

1. Full prior-art closure for the D-case

Especially determinants of between-cell scatter, finite relocation formulae, and exchange-stable partitions. This is the main novelty risk.

2. Global computational complexity

NP-hardness / parameterized complexity for variable \(d,K\); and how tight the fixed-parameter geometric enumeration is.

3. Population consistency

When do the empirical D-optimal quantizer and exact-exchange local optima converge to population objects as \(N o\infty\)?

4. Atomic score distributions

For atomless laws, purification removes the need for randomization. With atoms, the tightness of the deterministic versus randomized relaxation remains open.

5. Nuisance parameters / Ds

Population first-order geometry can be expressed through the efficient score, but finite exact geometry no longer matches the D-case. It needs its own theory.

6. E-optimality

The population objective has a common-supergradient intuition, but finite exchange geometry and representability remain unresolved.

7. Estimated score oracle

How do noise, classifier miscalibration, and finite training data propagate into the selected bins and retained Fisher information?

8. Distribution shift and \( heta_0\)

The score is a local object. How can the quantizer be transferred robustly across reference parameters or under covariate/model shift?

9. Stronger neighborhoods

2-exchange, merge/split operations, centroid moves, and hybrid global/local search may substantially reduce the gap to the global optimum.

10. Differentiable vs combinatorial

When does exact discrete exchange outperform smooth GMM/soft assignments, and when is the reverse true? This is a natural benchmark against GATO/BOBR/INFERNO.

7. Existing software: which part of the problem does each tool solve?

No package we found matches our formulation end-to-end. It is more useful to view the available tools as occupying different points in the pipeline “obtain a score → construct summaries/categories → perform statistical inference.”

MadMiner

Python · MIT · active research toolkit
score/SBI

A large simulation-based inference toolkit for particle physics; it includes SALLY/SALLINO and other score-/ratio-based methods. It is useful as a source or estimator of informative summaries, but not as an optimizer of hard D-optimal bins.

Overlap with our problem: score layer, not bin optimization

GitHub · PyPI

INFERNO

research code · BSD-3-Clause
inference-aware

Differentiable neural optimization of summaries/bins using an inference-motivated loss derived from a binned likelihood. A very important conceptual comparator, but not an exact hard quantizer and not a specialized D-logdet solver.

Overlap: downstream statistical objective; different optimization model

Code · paper PDF

ThickBrick

Python · MIT · PyPI 0.1.1 (2020)
categorization

A public implementation of optimal event selection/categorization from Matchev–Shyamsundar. It optimizes analysis sensitivity and has a Lloyd-like iterative structure. Algorithmically close, but its criterion is signal discovery/significance.

Overlap: hard categories + iterative optimization

PyPI · paper PDF

GATO

Python · TensorFlow · PyPI · 2026
closest modern

Gradient-based cATegorization Optimizer: multidimensional discriminants, a GMM/sigmoid bin model, differentiable approximation, and an objective based on binned-likelihood signal significance.

Overlap: multidimensional bin-shape optimization

PyPI · GitHub · paper PDF

BOBR

Python · Optuna/TPE · 2026
closest modern

Bayesian Optimization of Bin boundaRies from the same “Learning to bin” work. It does not require differentiability and optimizes a flexible binning parameterization through Bayesian optimization.

Overlap: black-box bin optimizer; different objective

GitHub · paper PDF

OptBinning

Python · Apache-2.0 · v0.21.0 docs
supervised binning

Mathematical programming for optimal discretization of numerical variables against binary, continuous, or multiclass targets, including 2D variants and constraints. It is a mature binning toolkit, but the objective is not Fisher information.

Overlap: production binning infrastructure, not the statistical objective

Docs · GitHub

scikit-learn k-means / clustering stack

general-purpose baseline
baseline

For the normalized-trace objective after Fisher whitening, k-means is the mathematically natural baseline. For the D-objective it is a useful initializer, but not an optimizer of the actual target.

Overlap: Voronoi clustering machinery

ScoreQuant project

planned general-purpose library
target niche

Target niche: full-matrix D-optimal hard quantization, exact exchange gains, optional global certificates, an explicit separation between sample partition and deployable quantizer, and a score oracle supplied as data, a function, or a classifier proxy.

Direct match to our formulation
Practical differentiation of the library
Rather than adopting a HEP-specific workflow, the API should live at the score-oracle abstraction level. The same optimizer can then work with an analytical likelihood, simulation-based scores, precomputed score vectors, or classifier-derived surrogate scores.
