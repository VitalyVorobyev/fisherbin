# Walkthroughs

*Who this is for: a reader who wants one applied question followed end to end, with real
numbers, rather than a method described in the abstract.*

A walkthrough here takes a single applied question from the raw problem to a fitted result: the
data and where it came from, which of the three input routes it uses and why, the fit, the
numbers that came out, what they mean for the original question, and what the binning you would
otherwise have reached for would have retained on the same data. Each one ends with a measured
limitation rather than a summary, because the limitations are the part that is hard to find
elsewhere.

None of the numbers on these pages is typed by hand. Each is read from a committed evidence file
produced by the example it describes, and a test resolves every one of them back to the run that
produced it.

## Which one to read

Pick by the situation you are in, not by the domain.

**[You have a trained classifier and want to bin its output](./ratios)** — *the ratio door.* A
two-component mixture where the exact Bayes score is known, so the number the library reports
about an estimated score can be checked against what that score actually achieved. Read this if
you want to know how far to trust a classifier-derived score, and what the ratio-closure
diagnostic does and does not tell you. Synthetic by design: it is the only one of the four where
ground truth exists.

**[You are measuring a rate and a systematic is eating your sensitivity](./hep)** — *profiled
information with a nuisance.* Real LHC-like events, a signal-strength parameter of interest, and
a tau-energy-scale nuisance that moves ten of twenty-eight observables at once. Read this if your
problem has a nuisance parameter and you want to see what binning for the profiled criterion buys
over binning the classifier output, and how much of that gap depends on which baseline binning
you compare against.

**[You count cells or events into categories and want fewer bins](./flowcyt)** — *estimated
ratios on real data.* Real bone-marrow flow cytometry, six populations, a per-patient composition
to estimate. Read this if your scores come from a classifier trained on labelled reference data
and you need a small, fixed binning that generalises to new subjects. Its result is a negative
one, and that is the point.

**[Your score is known in closed form](./michelson)** — *an analytic score with an explicit
nuisance.* An interferometer phase measured against a fringe-frequency nuisance, on the NumPy
backend. Read this if you can differentiate your model by hand: it is the one walkthrough where
the comparison against the optimum is exact rather than estimated, and where a natural-looking
binning turns out to retain nothing at all.

## What each one uses

| Walkthrough | Input route | Data | Criterion | The thing it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Ratio door](./ratios) | Density ratios from a classifier | Synthetic, with exact ground truth | D-optimality | What an estimated score's self-reported number is worth |
| [HEP classifier](./hep) | Density ratios + a central log-ratio | Real events, FAIR Universe HiggsML | Profiled D<sub>s</sub> | Binning for the nuisance, and how much the baseline choice matters |
| [FlowCyt](./flowcyt) | Density ratios from a classifier | Real cells, FlowCyt benchmark | D-optimality | When hard bins are enough, and when they are not an improvement |
| [Michelson phase](./michelson) | An analytic score function | Deterministic quadrature | Profiled D<sub>s</sub> | Aliasing, and a theorem-backed refusal |

Every walkthrough links onward to its full record in the reference, which carries the derivations,
the complete sweeps and the diagnostics these pages only quote from. Each also hands off to the
[Browser Lab](/lab), which runs the same fit on the same score table in your
browser with nothing installed.
